# Knowledge Base: Landscape Analysis by Work

This document maps specific works of Jane Austen to the aesthetic and psychological landscape analyses performed in Emilia's research.

## 1. Pride and Prejudice
- **Focus**: The relationship between ownership and aesthetics.
- **Key Site**: Pemberley's grounds.
- **Analysis**: The "natural beauty" of Pemberley is not artificial; it is a refined version of nature that reflects Mr. Darcy's true character—authentic, substantial, and without ostentation.

## 2. Sense and Sensibility
- **Focus**: The contrast between emotional volatility and environmental stability.
- **Visual Theme**: The tension between the "wild" nature favored by Marianne (Romanticism) and the "ordered" nature of the social world.

## 3. Northanger Abbey
- **Focus**: The parody of the "Gothic" and the "Picturesque."
- **Analysis**: Catherine Morland's desire for "mysterious ruins" and "dark corridors" contrasts with the mundane reality of the abbey, highlighting the gap between literary fantasy and actual experience.

## 4. Mansfield Park
- **Focus**: The moral dimension of the landscape.
- **Analysis**: The "wild" parts of the estate represent a lack of moral discipline, whereas the "managed" parts represent social order and duty.

## 5. Persuasion
- **Focus**: The "Realism" of the landscape.
- **Key Site**: The coast of Lyme Regis.
- **Analysis**: The rugged, unforgiving coastline reflects the hardship of the naval life and the emotional resilience of Anne Elliot. It marks the transition from "decorative" beauty to "lived" reality.

## 6. Emma / Sanditon
- **Focus**: The influence of the environment on social aspiration and urban development.
